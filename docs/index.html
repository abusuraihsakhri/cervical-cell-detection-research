Retrospective Multi-Source Research · September 2026

A clearer way to evaluate cervical-cell detectors.

This study investigates how microscope field overlap, incomplete reference annotations, and label conversion assumptions alter the measured performance of lightweight object detectors across multi-center datasets.

Explore Key Findings → Download Weights (.pt) ↓

Research use only: These models and benchmarks are retrospective scientific tools. They are not medical devices and have not been validated for screening, diagnosis, or clinical patient-level decision making.

The Evaluation Chain

  1. 01 Trace Source Fields Detect and group microscopically overlapping field pairs across images before splitting.
  2. 02 Audit Reference Completeness Compare model outputs against both native sparse labels and 40 dense human-verified fields (1,067 cells).
  3. 03 Decouple Operating Points Select operating thresholds on 20 development fields, then freeze for 20 reserved fields and 1,077 external HMCHH fields.

Study Architecture

Splits and references define the measurement.

Six YOLOv8n object detectors were trained under controlled protocols: either on a SIPaKMeD derivative alone or combined with APCData, across three matched seeds (17, 43, 101). Field overlap components were constrained to single partitions, followed by threshold selection on 20 development fields and evaluation on 20 reserved dense fields and 1,077 external HMCHH fields.

Empirical Results

Measurement choices changed the story.

When evaluated strictly against human-verified dense references and external centers, common assumptions in cell detection literature break down.

Finding 01 / Field Overlap

Filenames were insufficient.

Automated feature matching and photometric verification revealed 791 overlapping field pairs. A standard filename-grouped split would have allowed 10 of the 20 dense evaluation fields to cross partition boundaries, inflating apparent generalization.

Finding 02 / Reference Completeness

Sparse labels distorted both metrics.

On the exact same predictions on the 20 evaluation fields, abnormal precision measured 52.4–87.1% against the dense reference, but dropped to 20.4–45.7% against sparse annotations because unannotated cells were penalized as false positives.

Finding 03 / External Generalization

Cross-center transfer remained low.

On external HMCHH fields, detectors recalled 20.8–51.8% of annotated abnormal cells. Reference-matched precision ranged between 3.8% and 6.7%, highlighting significant domain shift across preparation and staining protocols.

How to interpret this research

This study uses publicly accessible microscope datasets. The binary classification maps Bethesda categories (APCData) and morphological classes (SIPaKMeD) into abnormal/normal groupings; the human-verified dense reference was reviewed by a single reader from proposal candidates; true patient-level identities were not disclosed in public sources; and external HMCHH labels cover abnormal cells only. This project investigates measurement validity and does not establish a screening threshold for clinical practice.
